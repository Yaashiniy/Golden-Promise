# Civil / Registry Wedding — Cultural Knowledge Vault

## Core Identity
- A **civil marriage** in Malaysia is registered under the **Law Reform (Marriage and Divorce) Act 1976 (LRA 1976)**
- Applicable to **non-Muslim Malaysians** (Chinese, Indian, Christian, and others)
- The marriage is registered at the **Jabatan Pendaftaran Negara (JPN)** — National Registration Department
- Civil weddings can be **minimal and intimate** (just the registry) or paired with a large celebration dinner
- Increasingly popular among modern couples who prefer a **neutral, non-religious** ceremony
- Guest counts vary widely: from **2 witnesses only** (pure registry) to **200+** (registry + reception dinner)

---

## Legal Process at JPN

### Step 1 — Notice of Marriage (Notis Perkahwinan)
- Submit a **Notice of Intended Marriage** to JPN at least **21 days before** the wedding date
- Both parties must appear in person
- Documents required:
  - **MyKad (IC)** for both parties
  - **Birth certificates**
  - **Single status declaration (Surat Akuan Bujang)** — statutory declaration confirming neither party is currently married
  - If previously married: **divorce certificate (Perintah Perceraian)** or **death certificate of previous spouse**
  - **Passport** (for non-citizens)
  - **Two witnesses' ICs** (witnesses must be present on the wedding day, not necessarily at this stage)
- Pay the **registration fee** (approximately RM30–RM50)

### Step 2 — Wedding Day (Solemnisation)
- Appear at the **JPN office** on the confirmed date
- **Two witnesses** must be present (any adult, not a family member — though in practice, JPN often accepts family)
- The **Registrar of Marriages** officiates the marriage
- Exchange of vows is brief and procedural — not ceremonial
- **Marriage certificate (Sijil Perkahwinan)** is issued on the day or within a few working days

### Optional: Solemnisation Ceremony Outside JPN
- A **licensed solemniser** (registered with JPN) can officiate the marriage at a venue of your choice
- Popular venues: hotel gardens, beachside resorts, home, restaurant private rooms
- The solemniser files paperwork with JPN after the ceremony
- This option gives a **ceremonial feel** while remaining legally valid
- Cost of solemniser: RM300–RM1,500+ depending on experience and travel

## Reception / Celebration

### Common Formats for Civil Couples
- **Intimate dinner**: 20–60 guests at a private dining room or restaurant
- **Garden party**: outdoor afternoon/evening celebration
- **Hotel ballroom**: for larger guest lists (100–300 pax)
- **Destination celebration**: Langkawi, Penang, Sabah, or overseas
- **Elopement + party**: couple elopes (just registry) then hosts a party weeks later

### Reception Elements
- **Couple's entrance** — grand march-in or casual walk-in
- **Welcome speech** by couple or emcee
- **Dinner** — Western set menu, buffet, or multi-course sharing
- **Toasts** — best man, parents, couple
- **Cake cutting** (if desired)
- **First dance** (optional)
- **Photo slideshow / video montage**
- **Photo booth**
- **Send-off** with sparklers, petals, or bubbles
- Civil weddings often skip religious elements entirely, making the reception more **flexible and personalised**

## Attire

### Bride
- **No cultural dress code** — full freedom
- Popular choices: white bridal gown, pastel suit, floral midi dress, jumpsuit, co-ord set
- For minimalist/modern weddings: structured blazer + wide-leg trousers in ivory or champagne
- Second outfit change is optional

### Groom
- **Suit** — navy, grey, or earth tones
- More relaxed than a church/ballroom setting; linen suits or smart-casual looks work for garden parties

### Theme
- Civil weddings lend themselves to cohesive **colour themes**: minimal white + green, terracotta + warm neutrals, dusty blue + gold, all-white, or dark romance

---

## Decor & Styling

### Popular Aesthetics
- **Minimalist / Scandinavian**: white, neutral tones, simple greenery
- **Garden / Botanical**: lush florals, earthy colours, outdoor setting
- **Romantic Luxury**: candles, draping fabric, ambient lighting, gold accents
- **Industrial Chic**: exposed brick, Edison bulbs, bare wood tables
- **Destination-Casual**: rattan furniture, tropical plants, natural light

### Key Decor Items
- **Floral arch or geometric backdrop** for ceremony/photo-taking
- **Table centrepieces** — floral or candle arrangements
- **Neon sign** ("Mr & Mrs", custom quote) — very popular at modern civil weddings
- **Seating chart / escort card display**
- **Welcome sign** at entrance
- **Guest book** — traditional, polaroid-style, or fingerprint tree

---

## Vendors to Book

1. **Licensed solemniser** (if not at JPN office)
2. **Photographer** — civil weddings are intimate; a skilled photographer matters more than a large team
3. **Videographer** (optional but recommended)
4. **Venue** — restaurant, hotel, or garden space
5. **Florist** — bouquet, arch, table arrangements
6. **Wedding cake** (optional — could be a dessert bar instead)
7. **DJ or live acoustic musician** for reception
8. **Hair and makeup artist**
9. **Emcee** (optional for intimate weddings)
10. **Wedding planner / coordinator** — helpful for day-of logistics even at small weddings

---

## Budget Notes
- Civil weddings are typically **the most budget-friendly** wedding format
- Major savings vs religious weddings: no religious ceremony costs, no mandatory cultural items
- Money can be redirected to: **high-quality photography, memorable venue, great food**
- Honeymoon is often a higher priority spend for civil couples — budget for it early
- JPN registration fee: ~RM30–RM50 (extremely affordable legally)
- Solemniser fee: RM300–RM1,500 (if choosing venue-based ceremony)

---

## Common Additions (Cultural Mix)

Many civil couples in Malaysia incorporate **elements from their heritage** even without a religious ceremony:
- Chinese civil couples: tea ceremony with parents (separate from the JPN ceremony)
- Indian civil couples: garland exchange and parents' blessing
- Mixed-culture couples: personalised vows that blend both backgrounds
- Malay Muslim couples cannot have a civil wedding under LRA — they must follow **Islamic law (solemnisation by Kadi)**

## Timeline Checklist Hints
- **6–9 months before**: Book venue, photographer, solemniser, florist, caterer
- **3–6 months before**: Submit Notice of Intended Marriage at JPN (must be at least 21 days before wedding day)
- **3 months before**: Confirm guest list, design seating chart, order wedding cake
- **1–2 months before**: Send invitations (digital or physical), finalise décor, confirm all vendors
- **2–3 weeks before**: Prepare all JPN documents, confirm witnesses
- **Wedding week**: Dry run at venue (if doing solemniser), delegate tasks, enjoy the week
