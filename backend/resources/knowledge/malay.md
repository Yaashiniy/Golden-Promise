# Malay / Muslim Wedding — Cultural Knowledge Vault

## Core Identity
- Malay weddings in Malaysia follow **Islamic law (fiqh)** under the couple's state's Islamic Religious Department (JAIS, JAKIM, etc.)
- The wedding is called **Majlis Perkahwinan**
- Guest counts are typically large: **300–800+ guests**, sometimes over 1,000 for prominent families

---

## Pre-Wedding Ceremonies

### Merisik (Enquiry Visit)
- Family of the groom visits the bride's family informally to gauge interest
- Not always practised in modern weddings but still common in traditional families
- Usually involves token gifts (fruits, kuih)

### Bertunang (Engagement)
- Formal engagement ceremony at bride's home
- Groom's family brings **hantaran bertunang** (engagement gifts) on trays — must be **odd number** (3, 5, 7)
- An engagement ring is presented
- A date for the wedding is agreed

### Malam Berinai (Henna Night)
- Usually held 1–3 nights before the wedding
- Bride has **inai (henna/mehndi)** applied to hands and feet by a **tukang inai**
- Close female relatives and friends attend
- Traditional music (kompang, joget) optional
- Light refreshments served

### Berinai Besar
- More elaborate henna ceremony; in some traditions held separately from Malam Berinai
- Bride wears a special outfit (usually baju kurung in jewel tones)

---

## Wedding Ceremonies

### Akad Nikah (Marriage Contract / Solemnisation)
- **The legally and religiously binding ceremony**
- Must be conducted by a **Kadi (Islamic marriage officer)** registered with the state religious department
- Requires: **Wali** (bride's male guardian — father, brother, or appointed wali hakim), two **male Muslim witnesses (saksi)**
- Groom recites the **ijab-qabul (marriage contract)** in a single breath — if broken, must repeat
- **Mas kahwin (dowry/mahr)** must be stated and paid — amount set by the state's religious department (e.g., Selangor: RM22.50 minimum, Johor varies)
- Registration at the Islamic Religious Department — couple must collect their **Sijil Perkahwinan (marriage certificate)**
- Can be held at a mosque, the bride's home, or a hall
- Held separately from bersanding — usually the morning or day before

### Bersanding (Enthronement Ceremony)
- The couple sits together on a **pelamin (dais/throne)** to be officially presented as husband and wife
- Guests come forward to **tepung tawar** (sprinkle yellow rice, rose water, and bless the couple)
- Accompanied by **kompang** drumming procession
- **Bunga manggar** (silver/gold tinsel flowers on poles) carried in procession
- **Bunga rampai** (fragrant flower petals) scattered
- **Sirih junjung** (betel leaf arrangement) displayed on the pelamin
- Bride and groom wear matching **baju kurung / baju melayu in songket or embroidered fabric**
- Typically split into two sessions: **Majlis Pihak Perempuan** (bride's side) and **Majlis Pihak Lelaki** (groom's side)

### Makan Beradab (Formal Dining)
- Symbolic first meal together as husband and wife
- Bride and groom fed by parents or elders
- Usually nasi minyak or nasi tomato with lauk pauk

---

## Hantaran (Gift Trays)
- Both families exchange gift trays — **must be odd in number** (7, 9, 11, 13 are common)
- Typical contents: wedding cake, shoes, handbag, cosmetics, tudung/accessories, fruits, sirih junjung, bunga telur, chocolates, jewellery
- Hantaran trays are **decorated elaborately** — hire a hantaran decorator or DIY
- The **wang hantaran (cash dowry)** amount is negotiated between families and presented separately from the mas kahwin
  - Typical range: RM5,000–RM50,000+ depending on the bride's education and family status
- Trays are displayed prominently at the reception

---

## Attire

### Bride
- **Baju kurung** or **baju kebaya** — often in **songket** (gold-threaded silk) or **lace**
- For akad nikah: usually white, cream, or pastel — covered and modest
- For bersanding: richer jewel tones — deep burgundy, emerald, royal blue, or gold
- **Sanggul (bun)** or **tudung/hijab** styled with flowers or gold accessories
- **Pending (belt buckle)** and **cucuk sanggul (hair pins)** in gold

### Groom
- **Baju melayu** with **sampin** (sarong worn over trousers)
- **Tengkolok/tanjak** (headgear) — varies by state (Johor tanjak differs from Terengganu)
- **Keris** (ceremonial dagger) sometimes worn at bersanding

---

## Food & Catering
- Traditional: **nasi minyak** or **nasi tomato** served with beef/chicken rendang, acar, dalca, sayur lodeh, and serunding
- Increasingly popular: **buffet style** for large guest counts
- **Air sirap bandung, air oren, teh tarik** as drinks
- **Kuih muih** (traditional sweets) for guests
- Dessert: **puding diraja, buah-buahan tempatan**
- No alcohol served — halal catering is mandatory
- Budget for caterer: RM18–RM35 per pax for buffet; nasi makan-beradab set can be more

---

## Venue
- Large **community halls (dewan), hotel ballrooms, or the couple's home compound**
- **Khemah (tent)** rental for outdoor home weddings — includes tables, chairs, lighting
- Pelamin (dais) is a separate vendor — custom-built or rented; prices RM3,000–RM15,000+
- Home weddings require road closure permit from local council (MBPJ, DBKL, etc.)

---

## Music & Entertainment
- **Kompang group** (Islamic drumming ensemble) for the procession — typically 12–20 men
- **Kumpulan nasyid** (Islamic vocal group) as a modern alternative
- Some families hire a **DJ or live band** for the reception (check local religious department rules — some states restrict music)
- **Dikir barat** in Kelantan/Terengganu contexts

---

## Key Vendors to Book
1. Kadi (Islamic marriage officer) — book via state religious dept, at least 3–6 months ahead
2. Pelamin decorator
3. Kompang group
4. Photographer + videographer (dulang/flat lay shots of hantaran are standard)
5. Makeup artist (MUA) — separate bookings for akad nikah and bersanding
6. Hantaran decorator
7. Caterer
8. Khemah/tent rental (if home wedding)
9. Bunga manggar supplier
10. Inai/mehndi artist

---

## Legal / Administrative Requirements
- Register intention to marry at the **state Islamic Religious Department** (e.g., JAIS for Selangor, JAKIM-registered state dept)
- Apply at least **21 days before** the wedding date
- Documents: IC (MyKad), birth certificate, single status declaration, photos
- Non-citizen marriages require additional documentation
- If previously married: divorce certificate (sijil cerai) required

---

## State-Specific Notes
- **Kelantan & Terengganu**: More conservative — music restrictions, gender-separated seating common
- **Johor**: Tanjak (headgear) culture is strong; royal-style bersanding common
- **Negeri Sembilan**: Adat Perpatih customs — matrilineal traditions still observed
- **Sarawak/Sabah**: Mix of adat and Islamic requirements — check local JAIS equivalent
- **KL/Selangor**: More liberal in entertainment; hotel ballroom weddings very common

---

## Timeline Checklist Hints
- **12 months before**: Book venue, pelamin, photographer, caterer
- **9 months before**: Register at religious department, confirm Kadi, book kompang
- **6 months before**: Order/commission baju pengantin, arrange hantaran items
- **3 months before**: Finalise guest list, print invitations (kad kahwin), book MUA, arrange bunga telur/door gifts
- **1 month before**: Confirm all vendors, prepare hantaran trays, collect sijil
- **Wedding week**: Malam berinai, rehearsal for akad nikah, final fittings
