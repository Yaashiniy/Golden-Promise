# Christian Wedding — Cultural Knowledge Vault

## Core Identity
- Malaysian Christian weddings span multiple denominations: **Catholic, Anglican, Methodist, Baptist, Evangelical, Charismatic, SIB (Sabah/Sarawak)**
- The marriage ceremony is conducted by an **ordained pastor, priest, or minister**
- In Sabah and Sarawak, Christianity is the majority religion — weddings often include indigenous Kadazan-Dusun, Iban, or Melanau cultural elements
- Guest counts: **80–400**; reception is typically a separate dinner event after the church ceremony

---

## Pre-Wedding

### Pre-Marital Counselling (PMC)
- Most churches require 3–6 sessions of **pre-marital counselling** before they will approve the wedding
- Sessions cover: finances, conflict resolution, family expectations, intimacy
- **Must be completed before the church will confirm the wedding date**
- Catholic churches additionally require **Pre-Cana course**

### Banns of Marriage
- Traditional practice in **Anglican and Catholic churches**: the couple's intent to marry is announced publicly in church on three consecutive Sundays before the wedding
- Allows anyone to raise a legal objection
- Less commonly practised in evangelical churches

### Church Booking
- **Book the church 6–12 months in advance** — popular churches have limited Saturday slots
- Confirm: church hall availability, ceremony duration, whether external decorators/florists are allowed inside the sanctuary
- Some churches restrict photography inside the sanctuary during the ceremony (flash, movement)
- Pay **church booking fee** and a **donation/love offering** to the pastor/priest

### Legal Registration
- In addition to the church ceremony, the marriage must be registered with **JPN (Jabatan Pendaftaran Negara)** for civil recognition
- The officiating pastor/minister must be a licensed **solemniser** registered with JPN, or a separate civil ceremony at JPN must be conducted
- Documents: IC, birth certificate, single status declaration, two witnesses' ICs

## Wedding Day

### Church Ceremony Structure
1. **Groom and groomsmen** arrive and wait at the altar
2. **Bridesmaids** walk down the aisle (processional music)
3. **Bride walks down the aisle** — escorted by her father (or both parents); traditionally to "Here Comes the Bride" (Bridal Chorus by Wagner) or a chosen hymn/contemporary worship song
4. **Welcome and opening prayer** by pastor/priest
5. **Worship songs** (1–2 hymns or contemporary Christian songs, led by worship team or organist)
6. **Sermon / homily** — brief message on marriage (10–20 minutes)
7. **Exchange of vows** — traditional or personalised; couple faces each other
8. **Exchange of rings** — "With this ring, I thee wed..." — ring bearer presents the rings
9. **Declaration of marriage** — "I now pronounce you husband and wife"
10. **First kiss** as a married couple
11. **Signing of register** — couple, witnesses, and officiating minister sign
12. **Recessional** — couple exits to celebratory music
13. **Photo session** outside the church with guests

### Wedding Party Roles
- **Best man**: holds rings, escorts groom, gives speech at reception
- **Maid of honour**: assists bride, holds bouquet during vows, gives speech at reception
- **Bridesmaids** (2–8): walk in processional, assist on the day
- **Groomsmen** (matching number to bridesmaids): usher guests, assist groom
- **Ring bearer** (young child): carries ring pillow
- **Flower girl** (young child): scatters petals down the aisle
- **Ushers**: guide guests to seats (bride's family traditionally on left, groom's on right)

## Reception / Dinner

### Format
- Held **separately from church** — hotel ballroom, function hall, garden, or restaurant
- **Cocktail hour** while couple does photography session
- **Grand march-in** of the couple with spotlight and upbeat song
- **Welcome speech** by best man or emcee
- **Dinner (buffet or set menu)**
- **Toasts** — best man, maid of honour, parents (optional)
- **Wedding cake cutting ceremony**
- **First dance** — couple's first dance as husband and wife (choreographed or simple)
- **Parent dances** — bride with father, groom with mother
- **Bouquet toss** (bride tosses bouquet to unmarried female guests)
- **Garter toss** (optional; less common in Malaysia)
- **Send-off** — guests line up with sparklers, bubbles, or dried petal confetti as couple departs

### Entertainment
- **Live band** or **DJ** for reception
- **Acoustic duo/trio** for cocktail hour
- **Photo booth** with props — very popular at Christian wedding receptions
- **Same-day edit (SDE) video** screened at reception
- Wedding games/activities during dinner

## Attire

### Bride
- **White bridal gown** — the cornerstone of a Christian wedding aesthetic
  - Styles: A-line, ballgown, mermaid, sheath, empire
  - Fabrics: lace, tulle, satin, chiffon
- **Veil** — cathedral, chapel, or fingertip length
- **Something old, something new, something borrowed, something blue** tradition
- **Wedding shoes** (often heels; flats increasingly popular for comfort)
- Second outfit change into a **cocktail dress or evening gown** for the reception is common

### Groom
- **Suit** (navy, charcoal, or black) — most common
- **Tuxedo** for more formal/evening weddings
- **Bow tie or necktie**
- Groomsmen often wear matching suits in a coordinated colour

### Bridesmaids
- Matching dresses (all same colour/style or "mix and match" in the same colour family)
- Popular palette: dusty rose, sage green, navy, champagne

## Key Items
- **Wedding rings** — exchange during ceremony; buy together at least 2–3 months ahead; can engrave with names/date inside
- **Wedding vows** — traditional or self-written (confirm with pastor which is preferred)
- **Ring pillow / ring box** for ring bearer
- **Flower arrangements**: church altar flowers, pew decorations, aisle runner, bride's bouquet, bridesmaids' bouquets, button holes (corsages) for groom and groomsmen
- **Wedding programme booklet** — order of service
- **Unity candle or sand ceremony** — symbolic blending ritual (optional)
- **Guest book** and pen station at reception entrance
- **Seating chart** and table numbers
- **Wedding favours** — small gifts for each table/guest: mini jams, chocolates, custom cookies

---

## Key Vendors to Book
1. **Church** + officiating **pastor/priest** — book early (6–12 months ahead)
2. **Reception venue** — hotel ballroom or function hall (9–12 months ahead)
3. **Photographer + videographer** (must be experienced in low-light church photography)
4. **Bridal boutique** — gown purchase or rental + alterations (6–9 months ahead)
5. **Florist** — church and reception arrangements
6. **Live band or DJ** for reception
7. **Caterer** (if not a hotel package)
8. **Wedding cake baker**
9. **Hair and makeup artist**
10. **Emcee / MC** for reception

## East Malaysia Notes (Sabah & Sarawak)
- **SIB (Sidang Injil Borneo)** church is dominant in Sabah — booking at SIB church requires membership or pastoral referral
- Kadazan-Dusun Christian weddings often include **sumazau dance** at the reception
- Iban Christian weddings may include **ngajat dance** and **tuak (rice wine)** at the reception
- **Gong music** is traditional and meaningful — a gonging ensemble may be hired
- Colourful **traditional costumes** may be worn for a cultural segment of the reception

## Timeline Checklist Hints
- **12 months before**: Book church, meet with pastor, start pre-marital counselling, shortlist reception venue
- **9 months before**: Book reception venue, photographer, videographer, live band/DJ, bridal boutique
- **6 months before**: First gown fitting, finalise florals, book wedding cake, design wedding programme booklet
- **3 months before**: Complete pre-marital counselling, send invitations, buy wedding rings, arrange seating chart
- **1 month before**: Final gown fitting, rehearse vows, confirm all vendors, prepare guest book + favours
- **Wedding week**: Rehearsal at church (run-through of processional), delegate day-of tasks to bridal party
