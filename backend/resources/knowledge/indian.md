# Indian / Hindu Wedding — Cultural Knowledge Vault

## Core Identity
- Malaysian Indian weddings are predominantly **Tamil Hindu** but also include Telugu, Malayali, Punjabi (Sikh), and Sri Lankan Tamil communities
- Each community has distinct customs — Tamil Hindu is most common in Peninsular Malaysia
- Weddings are officiated by a **Hindu priest (Vadhyar / Purohit)**
- Guest counts: **200–600+** for traditional weddings; modern intimate weddings can be 80–150
- The **muhurtham (auspicious time)** dictated by priest/astrologer governs the entire day's schedule

---

## Pre-Wedding Ceremonies

### Astrology & Muhurtham
- Consult a **Jothidar (Hindu astrologer)** to determine the **auspicious date and exact time** for the wedding
- The muhurtham window can be as short as 30–60 minutes — all key rituals must happen within it
- Horoscope matching (jathagam porutham) between bride and groom is standard
- Book the priest first; the date flows from the muhurtham calculation

### Nichayathartham (Engagement)
- Formal engagement ceremony
- Exchange of **betel leaves (vettrilai), fruits, and gifts**
- Groom's family presents saree, jewellery, sweets to bride
- Bride's family presents dhoti, shirts, sweets to groom
- Witnessed by both families; priest may officiate a short prayer

### Mehndi / Henna Night
- Held 1–2 days before the wedding
- **Mehndi artist** applies intricate henna designs to bride's hands and feet
- Bride's female relatives and friends also get henna
- Modern mehndi nights include DJ, dancing, and themed décor
- **Groom's name or initials** traditionally hidden within the henna design — groom must find it on the wedding night

### Seemantham (Baby Shower — if applicable)
- Traditional ceremony for first pregnancy, not directly wedding-related but sometimes mentioned in family context

### Nalungu / Seemantham Pre-Wedding Ritual
- Turmeric (manjal) applied to the bride by female relatives — purification and blessing
- Separate turmeric ceremony may also be done for the groom at his home

### Kaashi Yathra
- Playful pre-wedding ritual: groom pretends to "renounce the world" and leave for Kashi (Varanasi) as a monk
- Bride's father intercepts him, convinces him that marriage is the better path
- Groom is offered the bride and given a set of items (umbrella, fan, sandals, cane)
- A humorous moment — very photogenic

## Wedding Day Rituals

### Muhurtham Ceremony (Main Ceremony at Mandap)
- Conducted at a **decorated mandap (wedding altar)** — a canopied stage with fire pit (homam/havan)
- The **sacred fire (Agni)** is witness to all vows
- **Maalai Maathal (Garland Exchange)** — bride and groom exchange garlands (malai) 3–7 times; groomsmen lift the groom to make it challenging, bridesmaids lift the bride — a fun, competitive moment
- **Kanyadan** — bride's father formally gives the bride away to the groom; emotional ritual
- **Tying of the Thali (Mangalsutra)** — the most important ritual; groom ties the sacred gold chain (thali) around bride's neck; marks the marriage as legally and spiritually complete; must happen within the muhurtham window
- **Saptha Padi (Seven Steps)** — couple takes seven steps around the sacred fire, each step with a vow (prosperity, strength, wealth, happiness, children, long life, harmony)
- **Homam (Fire Ritual)** — offerings of ghee, rice, flowers, coconut into the sacred fire throughout the ceremony
- **Blessing from elders** — couple touches feet of elders (vanakkam); elders apply kumkum/tilak and give cash/gold

### Oonjal Ceremony
- Before the main ceremony: couple sits on a decorated swing (oonjal)
- Married women from both families sing traditional songs and feed the couple bananas and milk
- Symbolises the gentle swaying start of married life

### Reception / Seating
- After the muhurtham, a **reception line** where guests bless the couple
- **Wedding lunch (sadhi/virundhu)** — traditional banana leaf meal served to all guests
- Modern weddings: buffet with both vegetarian and non-vegetarian options

## Food & Catering

### Traditional Banana Leaf Meal (Sadhi)
- Rice served on fresh banana leaves with:
  - Sambar, rasam, kootu, poriyal (stir-fry), papad, pickle, payasam (sweet dessert)
  - Pappadum, appalam, chips
- **Fully vegetarian** for orthodox families; non-veg (chicken, fish, mutton) added for modern celebrations
- Water and drinks served by waitstaff going table to table

### Modern Additions
- **Briyani (Chicken / Mutton)** — very popular for Indian wedding receptions
- **Live counters**: dosai, idli, vadai, paneer dishes
- **Sweet table**: jalebi, gulab jamun, payasam, ladoo
- Caterer should be familiar with Indian wedding meal service flow

## Attire

### Bride
- **Saree** — traditional choice; typically silk (Kanchipuram / Pattu saree) in red, maroon, or pink with gold zari
- **Lehenga** — increasingly popular modern alternative; heavily embroidered
- **Bridal jewellery** — the most important investment:
  - Necklace (often gold with rubies/emeralds), earrings (jhumka), maang tikka (forehead piece), nose ring (nath), bangles, armlet (vanki), waist chain (oddiyanam), anklets (metti/toe rings)
  - Gold jewellery often gifted by both families — can be the **single largest wedding expense**
- **Bindi, kumkum** (forehead mark), kajal
- Flowers in hair: **jasmine garland (gajra)** wound through braid

### Groom
- **Dhoti + angavastram (shoulder cloth)** — traditional Tamil look; usually white or cream with gold border
- **Kurta + dhoti** combination also common
- **Sherwani** — for a more North Indian or Bollywood-influenced look
- Groom wears a **flower garland** and sometimes a **turban/sehra (floral headgear)**

---

## Jewellery — Special Notes
- For Indian weddings, gold jewellery is often the **#1 or #2 biggest budget item**
- Both the bride's and groom's families contribute gold
- Jewellery is often purchased at **Goldsmith shops in Little India (Brickfields, Klang, Ipoh)**
- **22-karat gold** is standard for wedding jewellery
- Sets can range from RM15,000 to RM200,000+

## Music & Entertainment

### Traditional
- **Nadaswaram** (double-reed wind instrument) + **tavil** (drum) — played during wedding procession and key rituals; essential at traditional weddings
- **Urumi melam** (percussion ensemble) for procession

### Modern
- **DJ** for reception and mehndi night
- **Live classical Carnatic singer** for sophisticated receptions
- **Bollywood/Tamil party songs** after dinner
- **Dhol player** (Punjabi influence) for fun procession photos

---

## Venue & Decoration

### Venue Types
- **Hindu temple halls** (sabha) — affordable, traditional
- **Banquet halls / hotel ballrooms** — for large modern weddings
- **Outdoor garden/resort** — growing trend for intimate weddings

### Decoration
- **Mandap (wedding altar)** — elaborately decorated with flowers (marigold, jasmine, roses), fabric draping, and pillars
- **Flower garlands everywhere** — entrance gates, mandap, stage
- **Kolam (rangoli)** — geometric rice flour patterns at entrance and mandap
- **Banana trees and coconut** — auspicious decorations at venue entrance
- **Diyas (oil lamps)** and brass urns (kindi/kamandalu) on the stage

## Key Vendors to Book
1. **Hindu priest (Vadhyar)** — book first, date is set around his availability and the muhurtham
2. **Photographer + videographer** — crucial; muhurtham rituals are fast-paced
3. **Mehndi/henna artist**
4. **Catering** (banana leaf, briyani, or full buffet)
5. **Nadaswaram group** (book early — in high demand)
6. **Mandap decorator / florist**
7. **Bridal makeup artist** (experienced with South Indian bridal look)
8. **Gold jewellery** — plan and purchase 3–6 months ahead
9. **Saree/lehenga** — buy or rent from Little India or bridal boutique
10. **Wedding hall / temple sabha**

## Tamil vs Other Indian Communities

### Telugu
- Similar to Tamil Hindu but with **Telugu-language mantras**; Pellikuthuru and Mangalasnaanam pre-wedding rituals
- Emphasis on **silk sarees from Andhra Pradesh**

### Malayali (Kerala)
- **Mappila (Muslim) Malayali** weddings have Islamic customs
- **Christian Malayali** weddings follow Church traditions
- **Hindu Malayali**: simpler ceremony, kasavu saree (off-white with gold border), often no nadaswaram

### Punjabi Sikh
- Ceremony at a **Gurdwara**
- **Anand Karaj** — four circumambulations around the Guru Granth Sahib
- Bride wears **salwar kameez or lehenga in red/pink**; groom in **sherwani + turban**
- **Langar (community meal)** — vegetarian food served to all guests
- **Bhangra and giddha** dancing at the reception

## Timeline Checklist Hints
- **12 months before**: Consult Jothidar for muhurtham, book priest, book venue and caterer
- **9 months before**: Book photographer/videographer, nadaswaram group, MUA, mehndi artist
- **6 months before**: Purchase/order gold jewellery, select saree/lehenga, design mandap
- **3 months before**: Print invitations, finalise banana leaf catering menu, confirm all vendors
- **1 month before**: Mehndi design consultation, rehearse ceremony order with priest, finalise Kaashi Yathra performers
- **Wedding week**: Nalungu (turmeric) ceremony, mehndi night, final fittings, garland preparation
