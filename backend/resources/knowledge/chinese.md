# Chinese Wedding — Cultural Knowledge Vault

## Core Identity
- Malaysian Chinese weddings blend **traditional Chinese customs** with modern elements
- Often two or three events: **betrothal ceremony, wedding day (ROM + gate crash + tea ceremony), and banquet**
- Can be Buddhist, Taoist, Christian, or non-religious in practice
- Guest counts: **100–500**, typically structured around **tables of 10**
- Dialect group influences customs: Hokkien, Cantonese, Hakka, Teochew, Hainanese

---

## Pre-Wedding

### Choosing an Auspicious Date (择日 — Zé Rì)
- Consult a **Chinese almanac (Tong Shu / 通書)** or a **feng shui master / fortune teller**
- Avoid the **Ghost Month (七月 Qī Yuè — 7th lunar month)** — considered inauspicious for weddings
- Avoid the couple's **zodiac clash years**
- Popular months: 2nd, 8th (prosperity), 9th (longevity), 12th lunar month
- The **date and time** of all ceremonies (gate crash, tea ceremony, banquet) may all be prescribed

### Guo Da Li (过大礼 — Betrothal Ceremony)
- Groom's family sends **betrothal gifts** to bride's family — usually 4–6 weeks before the wedding
- Gifts on red trays: whole roasted pig (全猪), canned goods (longan, lotus seeds, lily buds), wine, cakes (wife cakes / hopia), ang pow (cash in red envelope), jewellery
- Bride's family returns some items + gifts for groom's family
- The **bride price (pin jin 聘金)** amount is negotiated — can range from RM1,000–RM100,000+
- A **formal receipt (礼书)** of gifts may be documented

### An Chuang (安床 — Bed Installation)
- An auspicious day before the wedding, a **good luck person** (couple's children alive, not widowed) makes the bed with new red bedding
- Sweets, peanuts, red dates, longan placed on the bed
- No one should sleep in the bed until the wedding night
- Children may be invited to roll on the bed (for fertility blessing)

## Wedding Day

### Chuang Men / Zhui Men (闯门 — Gate Crashing)
- Groom and his groomsmen arrive at bride's home to "rescue" the bride
- Bridesmaids guard the door with **hilarious and embarrassing games/challenges** for the groom and groomsmen
- Classic games: eat bitter/sour/spicy/sweet foods, sing love songs, do push-ups, answer trivia about the bride, physical challenges
- Groom negotiates with **ang pow (red envelopes with cash)** to pass each challenge
- **Bride's shoes are hidden** — groomsmen must find or bargain for them
- The more games, the more entertaining for guests who watch via livestream or in person
- Finally, the groom presents the bride with the **bridal bouquet**
- This is a **signature Malaysian Chinese wedding moment** — worth dedicated photography and videography

### Hair Combing Ceremony (梳头 — Shū Tóu)
- Usually held early morning before gate crash
- Bride (and sometimes groom separately) has hair combed by an elder (parent or auspicious person)
- Four combing strokes with specific blessings:
  1. From start to end — complete togetherness
  2. Harmony till old age
  3. Many children and grandchildren
  4. Wealth and long life together
- Accompanied by recitation of blessings

### Fetching the Bride (接新娘)
- After gate crash, groom "fetches" the bride from her family home
- Bride bows or kneels to parents to say goodbye — emotional moment, important to capture on video
- Parents may give final blessings
- Bride carries a **red umbrella** held by a female relative as she exits (shields from bad spirits)
- Firecrackers (where permitted) or crackling sound effects as the couple departs

### Tea Ceremony (敬茶 — Jìng Chá)
- The most **meaningful and formal Chinese wedding ritual**
- Couple serves **Chinese tea (usually chrysanthemum or red date longan tea)** to elders in order of seniority
- Order: groom's parents → groom's grandparents → groom's uncles/aunts → bride's parents → bride's grandparents → bride's uncles/aunts
- Each elder receives a cup of tea, drinks it, and gives the couple an **ang pow or jewellery**
- Couple addresses each elder with the correct kinship title for the first time
- Held twice: once at the groom's home, once at the bride's home (or combined at venue)
- Tea set: buy a **dedicated double-happiness tea set** — red or gold themed

### ROM / Civil Registration
- Register at the **National Registration Department (JPN)** or a licensed solemniser
- Book a **solemnisation slot** at a hotel, garden, or venue if not doing JPN office
- Two witnesses required
- Official marriage certificate (Sijil Perkahwinan) issued

## Banquet (婚宴 — Hūn Yàn)

### Format
- **Chinese restaurant or hotel ballroom banquet** — tables of 10
- **10-course dinner** is traditional: cold appetiser platter, shark's fin soup (or yee sang alternative), prawns, whole steamed fish, roast duck/chicken, vegetables, tofu, noodles, dessert
- **Yam Seng (饮胜)** toasts — guests shout "YAAAAAM... SENG!" led by emcee
- **First march-in**: couple marches in to cheers and spotlight; often a second march-in after changing into evening wear
- **Cake cutting ceremony**
- **Bouquet toss** (optional, more Western influence)
- **Slideshow / video montage** of couple's love story
- Emcee hosts games and entertainment between courses

### Ang Pow Collection
- **Ang pow table** at entrance managed by a trusted family friend
- Guests contribute ang pow instead of physical gifts
- Customary amount: RM100–RM200+ per guest (should at least cover the "per head" cost of the banquet)
- Provide a **receipt book or digital tracker**

### Wedding Favours
- Traditional: **hard-boiled eggs dyed red** (buah telur merah), **peanut candy (candy-coated peanuts)**, or **egg rolls**
- Modern: customised cookies, mini cakes, scented candles, seed packets
- Placed at each table setting or in a goodie bag

## Attire

### Bride
- **Qun Kwa (裙褂)** — traditional red and gold embroidered two-piece gown for the tea ceremony (most important traditional look)
- **White wedding gown** — for the banquet march-in or solemnisation
- **Evening gown or second dress** — for the second banquet march-in
- Typically **3 outfit changes**: qun kwa → white gown → evening/cocktail dress
- **Gold jewellery set** (necklace, earrings, bangle) gifted by groom's family; worn during tea ceremony

### Groom
- **Western suit** (usually dark navy or black) for most ceremonies
- Optional: traditional **Tang suit (唐装)** for tea ceremony to complement bride's qun kwa

---

## Important Items to Prepare
- **Double-happiness (囍)** decorations and banners
- **Red candles** (dragon and phoenix candles) for the bedroom
- **Peanuts, red dates, longan, lotus seeds** — symbolic fertility foods
- **Red packets (ang pow)** supply for tipping vendors and distributing to children
- **Bridal umbrella** (red)
- **Corsages** for parents, VIPs, and key attendees
- **Photo booth** props — very popular at Malaysian Chinese weddings
- **Wedding video** montage or same-day edit (SDE) — standard expectation

---

## Key Vendors to Book
1. Restaurant/hotel banquet venue — book 9–18 months ahead; highly competitive
2. Photographer (gate crash + banquet) — book 9–12 months ahead
3. Videographer (same-day edit is popular)
4. Hair and makeup artist (3 looks)
5. Bridal package (gown rental/purchase + qun kwa)
6. Actual Day coordinator / wedding planner
7. Emcee (bilingual: Mandarin + English, sometimes dialect)
8. Florist (bridal bouquet, table centrepieces, ceremony arch)
9. Photo booth operator
10. Solemniser (if not at JPN office)

---

## Budget Cues
- Banquet venue is the **single largest expense** — RM1,200–RM3,000+ per table
- Gown package (including qun kwa): RM3,000–RM15,000
- Photography + videography: RM5,000–RM20,000+
- Gold jewellery set: RM5,000–RM50,000+ (family heirloom or new purchase)
- Gate crash ang pow budget for groom: RM500–RM2,000+

---

## Timeline Checklist Hints
- **12–18 months before**: Book banquet venue (high demand), shortlist photographer
- **9–12 months before**: Book photographer, videographer, MUA, bridal boutique
- **6 months before**: Send invitations, confirm menu with restaurant, consult Tong Shu for timing
- **3 months before**: Order/rent qun kwa, finalise guest list per table, prepare ang pow table logistics
- **1 month before**: Confirm all vendors, prepare gifts for guo da li, plan gate crash games
- **Wedding week**: An chuang (bed installation), hair combing prep, rehearse tea ceremony order
